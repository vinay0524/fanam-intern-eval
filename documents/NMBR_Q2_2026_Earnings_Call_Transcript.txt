Nimbus Robotics, Inc. (NASDAQ: NMBR)
Q2 2026 Earnings Call Transcript — July 22, 2026

Operator: Good morning and welcome to the Nimbus Robotics second quarter fiscal 2026 earnings call.
Joining us today are CEO Elena Marsh and CFO David Ochoa.

Elena Marsh, CEO:
Thank you. Q2 was a quarter of contrasts. Revenue grew 9.4% year-over-year to $412.6 million, ahead
of our guidance range, driven by strong demand for our collaborative arm product line, particularly
in automotive and electronics assembly customers. At the same time, we saw meaningful gross margin
pressure from input costs, which I'll let David walk through in detail.

David Ochoa, CFO:
Thanks, Elena. Gross margin came in at 38.2%, down five and a half points year-over-year. The primary
driver was a roughly 21% year-over-year increase in the combined cost of steel castings and
semiconductor components used in our servo drive assemblies. We also saw elevated freight surcharges
tied to ongoing carrier capacity constraints, which added a smaller but still meaningful drag.

Diluted EPS was $0.61 versus $0.89 a year ago. Of that $0.28 decline, we estimate about $0.19 is
directly attributable to gross margin compression from these input costs, with the remaining $0.09
split between higher interest expense on our revolver draw and increased R&D spend on our
next-generation vision-guided picking system.

On the balance sheet, we ended the quarter with $612.4 million in total debt against $498.1 million
in equity, putting our debt-to-equity ratio at 1.23x, up from 0.94x a year ago. That increase reflects
a deliberate decision to draw on our revolver to build inventory ahead of what we expect to be further
input cost increases in the second half of the year — it is not a reflection of deteriorating
profitability.

We're taking two concrete actions to address the cost pressure. First, we've qualified a second
semiconductor supplier in Southeast Asia to reduce single-source exposure. Second, we've locked in a
12-month fixed-price agreement covering about 60% of our steel casting volume starting in Q3. We
expect these actions to begin offsetting margin pressure in Q4, though we don't expect gross margin
to fully recover to prior-year levels until fiscal 2027.

Analyst Q&A — Marcus Webb, Fairview Capital:
Q: David, can you help us understand why margin didn't recover faster given you've had a couple of
quarters now to react to input cost inflation?
A (Ochoa): The steel and semiconductor cost increases were broader and more sustained than we
initially modeled at the start of the year. We didn't want to lock into a long-term fixed-price
contract until we had better visibility, which is why the fixed-price agreement only starts in Q3.
In hindsight we could have moved faster on supplier diversification, but we prioritized getting the
right long-term partner rather than the fastest option.

Analyst Q&A — Priya Nair, Ashcroft Securities:
Q: Should we read the revolver drawdown as a signal you're worried about further cost increases, and
how are you thinking about leverage going forward?
A (Ochoa): It's fair to say we built inventory defensively. We're comfortable operating up to roughly
1.5x debt-to-equity for a limited period, but we do expect to delever back toward 1.0x by the end of
fiscal 2027 as the fixed-price steel contract and new semiconductor supplier reduce the need for
precautionary inventory build.

Elena Marsh, CEO (closing):
To summarize: strong demand, real cost pressure, and a clear plan to offset it. We're confident in
the actions we've laid out and expect margin trends to inflect in the fourth quarter.

Operator: This concludes today's call.
